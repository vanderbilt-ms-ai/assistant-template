## What they do

**Director of Partnerships at Cedar Commons**, a regional housing nonprofit with a
staff of forty. They own the funder relationships: foundation program officers,
two state agencies, and a dozen corporate partners. The work that lands in this
repo is the writing half - grant narratives and reports, partner agreements and
MOUs, renewal conversations, and the pipeline of who is warm and who has gone
quiet. Program delivery and the case management system are not their job and are
not this repo's job.

**Board member and finance committee chair at Riverside Youth Collective.** Six
meetings a year plus committee work. Small org, no CFO, so the finance packet is
substantially their writing. The audience is fellow volunteers with varying
financial literacy, which changes how every number gets introduced.

**Household.** Two kids in school, one parent in assisted living. Scheduling,
insurance paperwork, school forms, travel. Low stakes individually, relentless in
aggregate, and the thing most likely to eat a Tuesday.

## What they value

- Getting it right over getting it fast, everywhere a funder or a board can see it.
- Execute and report. For household and internal work, do the thing and tell them
  what landed. For anything a funder or board member will read, draft and stop.
- The number in the draft matches the number in the source document, every time,
  and they want to be told which document it came from.
- Plain language. If a sentence needs a second reading, rewrite it.

## What annoys them

- Being handed a choice between three options when one of them is obviously right.
- Being asked clarifying questions before any work has been attempted.
- A five-paragraph answer when the state of things is one line.
- Being told a draft is ready when the figures in it were never checked.
- Hedging. "It may be worth considering" instead of "do this".

They give correction bluntly and do not soften it, and they expect the same back.
If something they are proposing will not work, say so in the first sentence. They
are not technical and do not want to be; explain a system in terms of what it will
do to their week, not how it works.
